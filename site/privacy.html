What Normal Reviews shares

This is a private beta for a small group of testers. Here’s exactly what happens with your data.

Stays on your phone, never uploaded

  • Your Google Timeline file and everything in it
  • Where you stopped, when, and for how long
  • Stops that aren’t restaurants, and visits you haven’t rated
  • Your home and work
  • Android app: if you turn on automatic visit detection, your phone checks each 20+ minute stop against the restaurant map. Restaurant visits become cards on your phone; every other stop and every raw location is forgotten right away.

To find which stops were restaurants, the app downloads a free map of restaurants for each area you’ve been to, in squares about 25 km wide, and does the matching on your phone. The website can tell which squares were downloaded (roughly which metro areas), but never your stops, times or the places you went.

To name your Timeline stops, your phone sends Google the place IDs from your own Timeline export (only for stops near a restaurant; never home or work). Google already has your Timeline. When a stop is a restaurant, the app shares a link between Google’s place ID and the restaurant on our open map so other testers don’t have to look it up again. Links never include who visited.

Shared when you rate a restaurant

  • Which restaurant, and its location
  • The day of your visit (never the time)
  • Your rating, and any comment, problem reasons or photo you add

Photos are shrunk on your phone first, which also removes the hidden location data cameras store in them.

Who sees it

  • Friends (people who added your friend code, or whose code you added) see your name next to your ratings.
  • Everyone else in the beta sees your ratings as “A diner,” with only a rating count and whether you tend to rate high or low.
  • Your email is used only to sign in. Other testers never see it.

Your controls

  • Undo a rating to remove it from everyone’s view.
  • Friends tab → Delete my shared data removes all your ratings, comments, photos and friendships from the shared database.
  • Prefer to keep it private? Tap “Not now” at sign-in and the app works just for you.

Where it’s stored: a Supabase database run by the app’s creator. Questions or deletion requests: ask the person who invited you.
Restaurant data: Overture Maps Foundation and © OpenStreetMap contributors.
